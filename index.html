How to use this score app?

From writing a chord chart to sharing it with your worship team — here's everything the app can do.

  • Your cloud library
  • Save to Cloud — keeps the score in your own account (you sign in once). An amber dot on the button means there are unsaved changes; leaving the editor then asks Save & leave / Leave without saving / Cancel.
  • Versions of a song — the pill next to the title lists every arrangement of that song: switch between them, add + New version, rename one, or give one its own YouTube link (✎). The link of the arrangement you are editing also appears as a ▶ chip in the topbar (click to watch — a saved start time like ?t=3214 is kept — and ✎ to change it), and an arrangement without a video shows a quiet No video pill (click it to paste one) in the same spot, so you always know which version has a video. Title, artist, key and time signature are shared by all versions of the song.
  • Share & import — in My Songs, Copy Link (🔗) on a card puts a share link on your clipboard; anyone who pastes it into Attach Link gets the song in their own library. New Song and Attach Link belong to the My Songs tab (they hide while Albums is open).
  • Albums — share with your team
  • Create or join — New Album creates an album with an invite code (rotate it any time from Invite); teammates join with Join Album. Everyone uses their own account, so the album stays curatable. Hover an album card for 📝 Edit details to change its name and description (MDs only).
  • MD vs Member — the MD (Music Director — the app's name for an album owner) invites people, adds songs, promotes a member to MD and can remove songs; a Member only reads. The last MD can never be demoted or removed, so the album can never be left without one. Anyone — a member or an MD — can walk away with the red Leave button in the album header; the last MD is asked to promote another MD first (or delete the album).
  • Add from My Songs — copies the chosen song into the album together with all of its versions; the album holds its own copy and your library is untouched. New Song inside an album creates a brand-new song there instead.
  • Member read-only canvas — as a member you can still change Key, Time signature, BPM and transpose (handy for rehearsal), while chords, lyrics, bars and sections are locked. Use Save a copy to get your own editable copy in My Songs.
  • Writing the score
  • Three writing modes — + New Song asks for the first arrangement's name, then shows three cards (each with a small live preview): 🎸 Chords (C, Am7, G/B with live suggestions), 🔢 Numeric Notation + Lyrics (1–7 with high/low octaves, half-step ♯/♭ and a lyrics field under every beat) or ♬ ChordPro (just lyrics with chords in [brackets] — the easiest one, no rhythm notation). The mode badge sits next to the title; in Chord Chart mode you can also show letter chords above the numbers.
  • ♯ Half-step numbers (naik ½ / turun ½) — in Numerical Notation (and in the number row of Chord Chart) a degree can be raised or lowered a semitone. Type the accidental first — #1 to raise, b1 or ♭1 to lower (the popover also shows three chips ♮ ♯ ♭ for touch screens) — then pick the octave you want: 1, high 1̇, low 1̣ — and with an accidental, the same three forms of that family (#1, #1̇, #1̣). The chips filter the suggestion list, which therefore always shows exactly what you are about to place (♮ → 1, 1̇, 1̣; ♯ → #1, #1̇, #1̣; ♭ → ♭1, ♭1̇, ♭1̣) — a crossed number is never mixed into the plain list. On the score, a raised degree prints as the number crossed by a slash rising to the right (/) and a lowered one as a slash falling to the right (\) — the notation Indonesian not-angka books use, drawn boldly and a touch longer than the digit so the coret is unmistakable. Playback follows the notation: a raised number sounds one semitone higher, a lowered one a semitone lower, in the octave you wrote (a lowered 1 really does sound below the tonic). The mark is drawn by CSS without taking any width, so the printed bars, barlines and pagination stay exactly as they are.
  • Chords above the numbers — in Chord Chart mode every section has its own Chords On/Off switch in its heading. Turn it on and a chord row opens above every beat of that section: write the Nashville number on the beat (type 1, 5, 6m, 1̇, ♭7) and its chord above it (C, G, Am) — you read the melody and the harmony on the same line, and ▶ Play sounds both together. It is per section on purpose, so turn it on only where it is needed. The row transposes with the key, follows rhythm subdivisions and prints with the score, while bars where it is switched off keep their height so the barlines stay lined up.
  • ♬ ChordPro, step by step — choose ♬ ChordPro in the New Song dialog and the editor opens with two ready sections: Intro ([C] [Am7] [Dm7] [G7] [Cmaj7]) and Verse ([C]Type your lyric here…). Type chords inside [brackets] straight in the lyrics — [C]Ama[G]zing grace — and the preview on the right updates as you type. Use {sov}/{eoc} (or {soc}) for a section header, {c: play softly} for a comment, and a bracket-only line like [Chorus] becomes a section label. There is no chord palette and no rhythm notation here — typing brackets is the whole point — and everything else still works: rename or reorder sections (↑ ↓), add more with + Add section, transpose the whole song (chords and key), and Export .pdf prints exactly what the preview shows.
  • Add or change a chord — click an empty beat and type it (pick a suggestion or press Enter); click a placed chord to retype it, or clear the field and confirm to remove it. Typing a slash sets the bass in one go, like D/F#. A chord the palette doesn't list is fine too: type it your way (Bm7♭5, Bmaj9, B6/9) and pick your own spelling at the top of the list — when nothing matches at all, Use custom chord (or Enter) keeps exactly what you typed. Custom chords transpose with the key and print like any other chord.
  • Split the rhythm — right-click a beat (long-press on touch) to split it into ½, ⅓ or ¼ beats when the chords change faster than the beat.
  • Fermata (hold) — right-click a beat (long-press on touch) → Fermata (hold)… and type how many extra beats to hold before the next beat plays. The note is sustained and the metronome keeps ticking on each held beat, so the pause stays in tempo — but it only affects playback; the score and the printed PDF just show the fermata sign above the chord or number.
  • Bars & sections — + Add 1 bar and × live under each section, click a section name to rename it, and + Add section in a section's tools inserts a new part right after it (the footer button still adds at the end). Select a section, then press Play in the topbar to start from that section; the same button turns into Stop while playback is running. Copy a bar (⧉) and paste it (⎘) somewhere else, or use a section menu (•••) → Copy bars to grab a whole range at once.
  • Key, time signature & transpose — click the key, the time signature or the song title in the header to edit them inline, and use ▲ / ▼ beside the key to shift every chord up or down a semitone. Choose 1/4 for a single-beat bar and the score is drawn as one growing bar: no bar line between the beats, but every line of the score still opens and closes with one — every beat you add just extends the line, the editor counts in beats (+ Add 1 beat), and no bar numbers are printed.
  • Lyrics — turn lyrics on for the song (or for a single section) to get a field under each beat; paste a whole line into the first field to spread the words across the beats, or use Auto-syllable to split a line into singable syllables.
  • Undo & redo — the Undo / Redo buttons or ⌘Z / Ctrl+Z and ⌘⇧Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z (50 steps). Alt+1…5 switches the toolbar tabs and Esc clears a selected chord.
  • Paper & sound
  • Export .pdf — the dialog previews the printed page live; chord size, lyric size, beat spacing, paper, margins and the Bars → Bar numbers stamp above the barline (Off / Line starts / Every bar, on by default, Chord Chart scores only) are remembered per song, so adjusting one sheet never changes another.
  • ▶ Playback & tempo — ▶ plays the score at the tempo saved with the song (each song keeps its own BPM, not a fixed 120). If it is still playing when you leave, the app asks first and stops it for you.
